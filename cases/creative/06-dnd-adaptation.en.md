# Adapting D&D 5e to the custom world of Echelon Beyond

**Type:** Creative R&D, rules hacking
**Section:** creative
**Role:** Game designer, rules hacker, worldbuilder
**Year:** 2026
**Status:** completed (adaptation materials are ready)

## Summary

Porting the D&D 5e system onto an original world: instead of ready-made races, classes and bestiary — custom ones
built for the lore of Echelon Beyond. The rules and balance of D&D are kept as the frame, while the content of the
system is rewritten for the world. In addition, official D&D adventures were studied, game mastering principles
were extracted from them, and the first session in the new world was run.

## Problem

Ready-made D&D 5e rules do not match the world of Echelon Beyond: races, classes, bestiary and magic do not fit
the author's lore. Playing "by the book" means putting someone else's setting on top of your own universe, while
dropping the system entirely means losing the balance the rules were tuned for. The task was to keep D&D's game
balance but rewrite the content of the system for the world: races, classes, monsters, items and anomalies.

## Solution

1. Four races were created for the world: Kautsy, Renary, Ursari, Sumerechniki.
2. Four new classes and subclasses were proposed: Admiral, Navigator, Engineer, Illusionist.
3. Modules, artifacts, viruses and anomalies were adapted to D&D mechanics.
4. A bestiary was compiled: Shard of the Devourer, Keeper of the Ripple, Matriarch, Heart of the Abyss and others.
5. Five or more official D&D adventures were studied (Lost Mine of Phandelver, Curse of Strahd, Tomb of
   Annihilation, Ghosts of Saltmarsh, The Wild Beyond the Witchlight) — game mastering principles were extracted:
   start small, a living antagonist, the world does not bend to the player, genre variety.
6. The first game session was run: the character Liss "Trailseeker" (a ranger-guide, Sumerechnik), a scene in the
   Sleeping Wanderer tavern, with NPCs played out (Greta, Harg, a korkhit, a renara).

## Skills demonstrated

- Rules hacking: adapting a third-party rules system to an original world while keeping balance.
- Worldbuilding: races, classes and a bestiary that grow out of the lore rather than generic fantasy.
- Tabletop game design: classes with roles, a bestiary by level, items and anomalies.
- Narrative design: scenes and NPCs that serve the world.
- Session preparation: a character, a scene, played-out NPCs, analysis of game mastering principles.
- Prompt engineering: working with an LLM as a co-author while reworking a large volume of rules.

## Tools

LLM chat models, the AI assistant Lorien, Cline, D&D 5e (SRD), Markdown.

## Limitations

- Playtests were not recorded and no statistics were collected.
- The bestiary is not numbered by level.
- Some stat blocks need manual editing to match the D&D standard.
- Only one session was run: the system has been tested in a narrow scope.
