# D&D module "Curse of Discord in the Snowy Valley"

*(Russian: «Проклятие Раздора в Снежной Долине»)*

**Type:** Self-initiated / AI-assisted / Creative R&D
**Section:** creative
**Role:** Narrative designer, worldbuilder, game designer
**Year:** 2026
**Status:** completed (module text and materials are ready)

## Summary

An adventure for D&D 5e characters of levels 1–5 with a winter theme, a moral dilemma and a branching plot:
snow goblins have stolen the Heart of Winter Balance, and the spirits Klaus and Grinch are feuding. The core
idea: the goblin leader Grall is not a villain but the father of a sick daughter, so the theme of the module is
reconciliation through understanding rather than victory over an enemy.

## Problem

Winter adventures almost always come down to "find the villain and defeat him": players get a fight but no
choice, and the moral dilemma becomes decoration. The table needed a module where the conflict can be resolved
by diplomacy as well as by force, where the antagonist has a understandable reason, and where the party's
decisions really change the ending. An extra requirement: it had to work both for an experienced group and for
beginners at low levels.

## Solution

1. A central conflict without a villain: two feuding forces (the spirits Klaus and Grinch) and the stolen Heart
   of Winter Balance as a reason to travel, not as a mere plot device.
2. An antagonist with motivation: the leader Grall stole the Heart not out of spite but to save his sick
   daughter — players can learn this and change their approach.
3. Three paths through the story: diplomatic, forceful, and one driven by unexpected twists (secret allies,
   a substituted culprit, an intervention by a third party).
4. Nine NPCs with roles in the plot: Klaus, Grinch, Jesper, Alva, Grall, Olaf, Bronislav, Marta and Lars, Ikik —
   each with a function and lines for negotiations.
5. Four locations with escalation: the Divided Bridge → the Chapel of Balance → the Ice Heart Caves →
   Grall's Throne Hall.
6. A custom mechanic, "Shards of Faith": a reward for kind deeds that can be exchanged for in-game bonuses
   (a dice re-roll, +1d4) — an economy of good behaviour instead of moralising.
7. D&D 5e integration: levels 1–5, compatible with the basic rules, ready stat blocks and skill checks for
   social scenes.
8. The winter theme as a setting rather than a greeting card: snowy valley, ice, light and balance rituals all
   work towards the main conflict.
9. Material for the game master: scene descriptions, NPC reactions, forks and ending variants.

## Skills demonstrated

- Worldbuilding: a snowy valley, spirits of balance and a winter setting that serves the conflict.
- Narrative design: branching, three paths and an ending that depends on the party's decisions.
- Antagonist design: Grall has a reason that makes "defeating him" the weaker option.
- Social scene design: negotiation as a full path, not a fallback.
- Working with D&D 5e rules: levels 1–5, skill checks, combat and non-combat encounters.
- Game economy of kind deeds: the "Shards of Faith" mechanic with exchangeable bonuses.
- Working with an AI assistant: generating, compressing and editing large texts under author control.

## Tools

LLM chat models, the AI assistant Lorien (drafts and edits), Cline (writing texts to files), Markdown, D&D 5e rules.

## Limitations

- No recorded playtests: encounter balance and check difficulty were judged by reasoning, not at the table.
- There are no ready maps, handouts or visual appendices.
- Some NPCs are described in text without stat blocks — the game master has to prepare them.
- Module versions were not numbered and the edit history was not kept.
- The module is unpublished: it is personal material for a table.
